title: Causes of the First World War
---

The First World War began in 1914 after the assassination of Archduke
Franz Ferdinand in Sarajevo, but historians distinguish long-term and
short-term causes.

Long-term classroom factors often grouped as MAIN:
- Militarism: arms races and war planning, including Anglo-German naval rivalry.
- Alliances: Triple Alliance and Triple Entente turning a regional crisis into a general war.
- Imperialism: competing empires, especially in the Balkans and Africa.
- Nationalism: in the great powers and among Slavic nationalists in Austria-Hungary.

Short-term: the July Crisis of 1914, Austrian demands on Serbia, German
support for Austria (blank cheque), Russian mobilisation, and German
invasion of Belgium which brought Britain into the war.

No exam board is assumed. Treat competing interpretations as historical
debate, not as a single official syllabus point unless a board is named.

Common misconception: the assassination alone "caused" the war. It was
the trigger within a system of alliances and militarised diplomacy.
