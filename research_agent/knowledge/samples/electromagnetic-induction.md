title: Electromagnetic induction
---

Electromagnetic induction is the generation of an electromotive force (emf)
in a conductor when the magnetic flux linkage through it changes.

Faraday's law states that the induced emf is equal to the negative rate of
change of magnetic flux linkage. In symbols, emf = -N ΔΦ / Δt, where N is
the number of turns and Φ is magnetic flux.

Lenz's law gives the direction: the induced current opposes the change in
flux that produced it. This is the origin of the minus sign in Faraday's law.

A-Level classroom points:
- Magnetic flux Φ = BA cosθ for a uniform field.
- Flux linkage = NΦ.
- A changing field, a moving conductor, or a changing area can induce an emf.
- Transformers and generators are applications, not the definition.

Common misconception: a constant magnetic field through a still coil does
not induce an emf. Change of flux is required.
