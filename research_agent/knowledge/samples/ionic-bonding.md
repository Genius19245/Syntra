title: Ionic bonding
---

Ionic bonding is the electrostatic attraction between oppositely charged
ions, formed when electrons are transferred from a metal atom to a
non-metal atom.

GCSE classroom points:
- Metals lose electrons to form positive ions (cations).
- Non-metals gain electrons to form negative ions (anions).
- The ions form a giant lattice, not simple molecules.
- Ionic compounds typically have high melting points and conduct electricity when molten or dissolved, but not as solids.

Example: sodium chloride. Sodium (2,8,1) loses one electron; chlorine
(2,8,7) gains one electron. Both achieve a full outer shell.

Common misconception: ionic compounds exist as separate NaCl molecules.
At GCSE they are described as giant ionic lattices.
